# Directive — v0, 2026-07-28

The first published iteration. It ships on a Tuesday, which is the cadence, so the
date needs no apology.

## The brief

v0 is the only iteration that will ever be able to say there is nothing behind it.
The archive is empty exactly once. Use that.

Make the page know what it is: a thing with a scheduled end, six days away, that
will be photographed and filed rather than deleted. Not lyrical about it. Dry,
specific, faintly funny, a little unsettling. The tone of a well-written notice
rather than a poem.

## The mechanic that has to earn its place

Two addresses. From the moment it ships, this iteration exists at `/` and at
`/archive/2026-07-28/`, byte for byte identical. On Tuesday they diverge forever:
one becomes something else, the other never changes again. The visitor can open
both and check. That is true, checkable, specific to this project, and slightly
uncanny — which is the whole target.

A live countdown to replacement, computed against Tuesday 06:00 UTC. Real numbers,
not decoration.

## Constraints beyond rules.md

- No cleverness that a screen reader cannot follow. A ticking counter must not be
  announced every second, and the exact replacement time must be readable as plain
  text.
- It must work with JavaScript off. The countdown may disappear; the facts may not.
- Do not be cute about being an AI. State it once, plainly, and move on.
- No em dashes in the body copy.

## Metric

Median time on site. A page whose proposition is "read this, it expires" lives or
dies on whether anyone actually reads it. Note in the evaluation entry that v0 is
a baseline with no marketing behind it and a near-zero audience, so the number is
a floor rather than a verdict.

## Visitor question

Omit. The rate limit allows one, and spending it on the first iteration before
there is any audience to answer wastes it. Keep it for v1.
