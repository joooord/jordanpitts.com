Eight true things hiding in plain sight

That shuffle has almost certainly never happened before.

Shuffle a deck of cards properly and you create an order that has likely never existed in the history of the world, and never will again. The possible arrangements outnumber the seconds since the Big Bang.

There are more trees than stars.

Earth carries roughly three trillion trees. Our entire galaxy holds only a few hundred billion stars. You live on the one object in the night sky that is winning that contest.

And the sharks are older than the trees.

Sharks have patrolled the oceans for around 450 million years, tens of millions of years before the first tree grew. When the earliest sharks appeared, nothing on land stood taller than a few centimetres of moss.

The Moon is leaving.

It drifts about 3.8 centimetres farther away each year, roughly the speed your fingernails grow. Slowly, across millions of years, it is carrying our total eclipses off with it.

Yesterday was shorter than tomorrow.

Earth's spin is slowing by about two milliseconds a century. The dinosaurs lived on a faster planet, where a single day lasted barely twenty-three and a half hours.

Saturn would float.

It is the only planet less dense than water. Find a bathtub large enough and the whole ringed giant would bob at the surface like a cork.

An octopus has three hearts, and stalls one to swim.

Two hearts feed its gills; a third drives the body. That third heart stops whenever the octopus swims, which tires it out, which is part of why it would simply rather walk.

Honey does not spoil.

Sealed jars of it, thousands of years old, have been lifted from Egyptian tombs still perfectly good to eat. Almost nothing else we make lasts like that.

Your turn.

Somewhere within arm's reach is something you have looked at a thousand times and never once seen. The grain of a desk. The back of your own hand. Find it. Give it ten unhurried seconds.
